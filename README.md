# Executive Summary

Hope Cement Works (Breedon Group, Derbyshire) is one of four sites in the Peak Cluster carbon capture and storage (CCS) project — a proposed £5bn, ~198km pipeline to transport CO₂ from Peak District cement and lime operations to offshore storage under the Irish Sea. This dossier assesses how Hope can most cost-effectively prepare its CO₂ stream for that pipeline

Breedon has publicly stated (March 2025 community update) that amine scrubbing — not oxy-fuel combustion — is “closest to realisation” for Hope, with disclosed internal cost estimates of approximately £400m for capture and £300m for CO₂ transport infrastructure. A public Environment Agency permit decision document confirms why: Hope's two kilns were installed in the early 1970s with a multistage cyclone preheater but no calciner, making a main-burner oxy-fuel retrofit a substantially harder and less-precedented undertaking than for a modern precalciner kiln.
This dossier does not contest that decision. Instead, it models the smallest capital package that meaningfully reduces the running cost of the amine plant Breedon has already chosen: (1) routing kiln waste heat directly to the amine reboiler's steam demand, (2) a De-SOx pretreatment step to protect the solvent from Hope's unusually sulphur-rich local shale, and (3) battery storage (BESS) to buffer the pipeline-compression electrical load.

Headline result: an estimated £13.9m incremental capital package returns £6.6m/yr in operating cost savings — a simple payback of approximately 2.1 years. A Monte Carlo sensitivity analysis across a coupled, sourced set of inputs puts the payback range at 1.5–3.8 years (P10–P90), with the optimized package beating the as-planned baseline in 99.9% of 10,000 simulated scenarios.
Both the waste-heat capex line and the De-SOx cost structure have since been rebuilt on real, sourced primary data (Sections 5–6), replacing two rounds of unbenchmarked placeholders. The result is a smaller, less dramatic headline figure than earlier drafts of this dossier showed — but a substantially more defensible one. The remaining uncertainty is now concentrated in a single, well-defined risk: a ~10% chance that a standalone SOx polisher proves necessary beyond the standard pretreatment already included in the £400m baseline.

# Verified Findings — Supplementary Notes

A short, standalone log of facts checked against primary sources since the original dossier and articles were published. Nothing here is a projection or a draft — each item below is independently sourced and confirmed. For the financial model and Monte Carlo analysis, see the main dossier and notebook in this repo.

## Peak Cluster project scale (correcting an earlier public figure)

Peak Cluster's own published figures state the project will cut **over 3 million tonnes of CO₂ per year** — not the 4 million tonnes cited in an earlier draft of related material. Source: [Peak Cluster, "Your Questions Answered"](https://peakcluster.co.uk/your-questions-answered/).

## Public investment to date

Direct public capital committed so far is **£28.6m from the National Wealth Fund** (announced July 2025), which unlocked over £30m of private co-investment from the project's JV partners and the four operators (Tarmac, Breedon, Holcim, SigmaRoc). The often-quoted £5bn figure is the *total* estimated project cost, most of which is expected to be private/consortium capital, not public money committed to date. Source: [National Wealth Fund news release](https://www.nationalwealthfund.org.uk/news-and-publications/news/national-wealth-fund-invests-in-peak-cluster-to-secure-a-resilient-future-for-cement-and-lime-manufacturing-in-the-uk/).

## Solar farm at Hope: capacity confirmed

Breedon's own public consultation materials for the Hope quarry extension state the proposed solar farm — sited in the old shale quarry — would have a **3–4MW generating capacity**, enough to power roughly 2,500 average family homes. Source: [Hope Extension and Solar Public Consultation](https://my.engaged.space/hope/).

## Graphene-enhanced cement: real trial result vs. design target

Breedon and First Graphene have run real, large-scale trials of graphene-enhanced cement (PureGRAPH-CEM additive) at Hope, producing ~600 tonnes in a single batch. The **design target** for the additive is up to 16% CO₂ reduction via lower clinker content; the **actual achieved result** in real-world trials was up to **14% cradle-to-gate CO₂ reduction** (and up to 8% cement reduction). Worth using the achieved figure, not the target, in any future analysis. Sources: [cemnet.com](https://www.cemnet.com/News/story/180223/breedon-and-first-graphene-launch-enhanced-cement-trials.html), [globalcement.com](https://www.globalcement.com/news/20234-first-graphene-and-breedon-produce-600t-of-graphene-enhanced-cement).

## Alternative Raw Materials (ARM) Project: real scope

Breedon's ARM project at Hope uses **Pulverised Fuel Ash (PFA) and slate blindings** (not just generic "slate") as substitutes for locally-quarried, high-sulphur shale. The planning application proposes importing **up to 450,000 tonnes/year** of ARM by rail. The stated primary driver is reducing reliance on shale (a declining, sulphur-rich local resource) — whether this also reduces process (calcination) CO₂, versus being primarily a sulphur/resource-security measure, has not yet been established and needs further research before being used as a decarbonisation claim. Source: [Don Valley Engineering Group, ARM Project case study](https://donvalleyeng.com/casestudies/arm-project/).

## The ICC business model mechanism (why self-funding measures matter)

The UK Government's Industrial Carbon Capture (ICC) contract — the subsidy mechanism relevant to Hope's amine plant — is a Contract-for-Difference-style structure, running up to 15 years (10-year initial period, extendable annually by up to 5 more), paying operators a per-tonne difference payment between a "strike price" (built from the operator's own opex, transport & storage costs, and capex repayment) and a market-linked reference price. Because the strike price reflects the operator's own cost base, an unoptimised operator locks in a higher strike price — and the public pays more, for longer, to cover it. Source: [DESNZ, Industrial Carbon Capture Business Models Update](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/cd2a55e2/industrial-carbon-capture-business-models).

## Capture technology options appraisal (new, Phase 2 draft)

A separate submission — `Hope_MOF_Options_Appraisal_Submission.docx` — argues that the choice of capture technology at Hope (liquid amine scrubbing by default) should be the subject of a formal, documented Options Appraisal against solid sorbent (MOF) technology, specifically Svante's CALF-20, before the DCO application and Environmental Statement are finalised. It is explicit that CALF-20 is not yet proven at Hope's likely scale — the only cement-specific precedent is a 1 tonne/day pilot (LafargeHolcim's CO2MENT Richmond project) against Hope's probable ~3,300+ tonnes/day requirement — and asks for a pilot-deployment and technology-readiness review point built into the consultation process, not an outright rejection of amines. See that document for full sourcing (CALF-20 steam ratio ~1.5 kg/kg CO₂, demonstrated capture cost ~$38/tonne against a $30/tonne target).

## Pipeline routing: why "follow the road or railway" doesn't hold up

See `PIPELINE_ROUTING_NOTE.md` for a short, sourced note on three real constraints on "common sense" pipeline routing: the permanent 6–25m maintained easement required for the life of the asset; Network Rail's Asset Protection (ASPRO) regime and equivalent highways-authority controls on anything crossing or running alongside live transport corridors; and the genuine asphyxiation hazard of dense-phase CO₂, illustrated by the 2020 Satartia, Mississippi pipeline rupture.
